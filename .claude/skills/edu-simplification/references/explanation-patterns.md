# Explanation patterns with good and weak examples

## Example first, then rule
- Weak: "Compound interest is interest calculated on the principal and accumulated interest."
- Good: "You put 1,000 in at 10% a year. After year 1 you have 1,100. In year 2 the 10% is earned on 1,100, so you gain 110, not 100. Earning interest on past interest is compound interest."

## Analogy with its limit
- "Menisci are like rubber washers between two bones: they spread load and add stability. Unlike washers, they are C-shaped, attached at their ends, and partly fixed to surrounding structures, so tears follow specific patterns."

## Step-by-step
Numbered, one action per step, each step checkable. End with a quick self-check question.

## Comparison table
Use for confusable items (simple vs compound interest; ACL vs PCL; T1 vs T2 appearance). Columns are the features that actually distinguish them, not everything known.

## Mnemonics
- Good: maps one-to-one to the content and is shorter than the list itself.
- Weak: forced acronyms with filler letters, or rhymes that encode a slightly wrong fact.
- Always present the content first; the mnemonic is a retrieval aid, not the explanation.

## Signaling simplification
"For now we assume yearly compounding; later you will see monthly compounding."

## Misconception pattern
"A common mistake is to add the interest rate once per year to the original amount only. That is simple interest. With compounding, each year's interest is calculated on the new total."
