# Source reliability guide

## Tiers
| Tier | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Primary authoritative | Standards bodies, government regulators and statistics offices, central banks, professional society guidelines, peer-reviewed systematic reviews, official curricula | Essential facts, definitions, rules, figures |
| 2 Established secondary | University course pages, recognized textbooks, NCBI Bookshelf (e.g. StatPearls), peer-reviewed reviews, major encyclopedias with editorial review | Explanations, context, cross-checks |
| 3 Expert community | Expert-edited wikis with references (e.g. Radiopaedia for imaging), reputable professional education sites | Examples, images, teaching angles; verify essentials against Tier 1 or 2 |
| 4 General | News, blogs, forums, AI-generated pages, commercial landing pages | Leads only; never the sole support for an essential claim |

## Red flags
No author or organization; no date or a date far older than the field's update cycle; selling a product; claims without references; contradicts Tier 1 sources; content farm patterns; machine-translated text in specialist fields.

## Recency guide
- Fast-changing: regulations, tax and interest-rate figures, clinical guidelines, software and AI: check the date, prefer the last few years, state the as-of date.
- Stable: mathematics, basic anatomy, physical laws: older authoritative sources are fine.

## Domain starting points (verify availability when used)
- **Personal finance and mathematics:** national financial-education and consumer-protection agencies (e.g. investor.gov from the US SEC, consumerfinance.gov), central bank education pages, university mathematics course notes, Khan Academy for teaching angles.
- **Medicine and radiology:** professional society education materials (e.g. RSNA, ACR, ESR), NCBI Bookshelf / StatPearls, PubMed-indexed reviews, Radiopaedia as Tier 3 with references, standard anatomy textbooks. Mark content as educational, not clinical guidance; flag items for clinician review.
- **Psychology and assessment:** APA and ITC guidelines, the Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing (AERA/APA/NCME), peer-reviewed psychometric literature.
- **Education science:** peer-reviewed reviews and meta-analyses; education research bodies (e.g. Education Endowment Foundation, What Works Clearinghouse).

## Conflicts
When Tier 1 or 2 sources conflict: present both, note dates, scope and population; prefer the newer guideline when it explicitly supersedes the older one; otherwise escalate.
