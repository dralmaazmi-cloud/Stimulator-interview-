# Educational research and fact verification

Goal: a small, reliable evidence base for the topic and learner level, with every essential claim traceable to a source you actually opened.

## Process
1. **Frame the question.** Topic, learner level, purpose (for example "16-year-old, first exposure, personal finance literacy"). List the 5 to 12 claims the lesson will depend on.
2. **Plan sources by tier** (see `references/source-reliability.md` for tiers, red flags and domain lists; read it when you start a new domain). Prefer Tier 1 and 2 sources.
3. **Search narrowly.** Use WebSearch with specific queries (concept + authoritative body or "guideline", "textbook", "site:.gov", "site:.edu"). Open candidate pages with WebFetch and read only the relevant part.
4. **Budget.** For a small demo, 3 to 6 sources in total are enough. For a full course, scale per module. Stop searching once each essential claim meets the verification rule.
5. **Verify.** For each essential claim: two independent Tier 1 or 2 sources agree, or one primary authoritative source with the limitation stated. Check publication or review dates; flag anything that may be outdated (guidelines, statistics, regulations, technology, rates).
6. **Record.** Fill the evidence log (`references/evidence-log-template.md`). Give each source an ID (S1, S2...) and tag each claim Documented, Inferred or Estimated.
7. **Report uncertainty.** List disputed points, gaps and items needing expert review. If a fetch fails or a page is paywalled, say so; do not cite what you could not read.

## Hard rules
- Never invent a source, URL, author, quotation, statistic or date.
- Do not cite a source from memory. If you know a classic reference but did not open it, mark the claim Inferred or open the source first.
- A search result snippet is not verification; open the page.
- Keep quotations short and attributed; summarize instead of copying.
