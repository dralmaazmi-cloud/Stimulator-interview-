# Instructional design and learning science

Goal: a logical, progressive learning path where every objective has teaching, practice and a check, and each technique is chosen for a reason.

## Process
1. **Learner profile.** Age or role, prior knowledge, goal, context (phone, minutes per session, language). State assumptions explicitly when not given.
2. **Objectives.** 3 to 7 per module, each with an observable verb at the right cognitive level (remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, create) and a condition and standard when useful. Example: "Given principal, rate and years, calculate the final amount with yearly compounding to the nearest cent."
3. **Prerequisites.** List what the learner must already know; add a quick check or a short refresher for anything uncertain.
4. **Sequence.** Default orders: simple to complex, concrete to abstract, whole-part-whole (overview, parts, integrated practice). Put prerequisites before the concepts that use them. Place a misconception right where it is likely to arise.
5. **Modules.** Group objectives into short modules (5 to 10 minutes on a phone). Each module: hook or question, explanation, worked example, practice with feedback, brief retrieval check.
6. **Adapt to expertise.** Novices need worked examples and guidance; experienced learners benefit from problems and less guidance (expertise reversal). Offer a placement check or skip option for advanced learners.
7. **Choose techniques per objective** with `references/learning-science-techniques.md` (read it when choosing). Write one line per technique: why it fits this objective.
8. **Output:** objectives table, prerequisite list, module map, and the technique rationale.

## Rules
- Select techniques because they fit the objective, never as a checklist to apply everywhere.
- Describe designs as "evidence-informed". Do not claim the resulting app is proven to improve learning; that needs measurement with real learners.
