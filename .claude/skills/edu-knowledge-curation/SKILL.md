# Knowledge curation and content prioritization

Goal: the smallest body of content that achieves the learning objectives, with nothing essential missing and nothing distracting kept.

## Process
1. **List candidates.** Every fact, concept, procedure, term and example found during research, one line each.
2. **Score each item** against the learner and objectives using `references/prioritization-matrix.md` (read it the first time in a task).
3. **Classify:**
   - **Essential:** required to meet an objective, or a prerequisite for something essential.
   - **Supporting:** improves understanding or transfer; include if the load budget allows.
   - **Optional / enrichment:** interesting but not needed; offer behind "learn more" at most.
   - **Exclude:** out of scope for this learner, outdated, unreliable, or misleading at this level.
4. **Budget cognitive load.** Aim for about 3 to 5 new essential ideas per short module or screen group. Move overflow to a later module or to supporting material.
5. **Preserve essentials.** Never cut an item that a later objective depends on; never simplify an item into something false.
6. **Document decisions.** Output a table: item, class, reason, source IDs. Add a short "deliberately excluded" list so reviewers can see what was left out and why.

## Signals that something is essential
It appears in most authoritative sources; errors about it cause real harm or wrong answers; later content builds on it; it is directly assessed.

## Signals that something should go
Historical detail with no bearing on the objective; edge cases experts rarely use; jargon that can be replaced by a plain term at this level; duplicated points.
